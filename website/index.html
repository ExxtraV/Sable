Chapter 2

The harbor bell rang twice before dawn. The tide was out, which meant someone had lied.

Marren pulled her coat tight and walked toward the water. The boats knocked together in the dark, and the Harbor kept its count.

She did not answer. Counting was how the harbor kept its dead.

First, a good Markdown editor

Everything stays in the way of the writing.

01

See the writing, not the marks.

Bold, italics, links, headings, lists, quotes, and code appear as you mean them, while the original Markdown stays yours.

02

Settle into the page.

Pick from six themes and five writing fonts. Adjust the width, spacing, zoom, and scrolling behavior until the page feels right.

03

Let the rest fall away.

Paragraph focus dims the surrounding text. The toolbar arrives only when you reach the edge. The writing desk can disappear with a gesture.

Six quiet themes

Choose the light that lets you stay longer.

Then, a home for the story

A project can be as simple as a folder.

Make a Fiction Project when one manuscript needs characters, locations, world notes, images, and a little more shape. Sable lays out the folder; the work remains plain Markdown.

  • Cards beside the page for characters, locations, and world notes.
  • Scene tags for the people and places in the chapter.
  • Manuscript view for word goals, chapter lengths, and reading order.
